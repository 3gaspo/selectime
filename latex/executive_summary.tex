\section*{Main findings}

On 90 matched TIME tasks, the best aggregate result is the frozen
\textbf{Chronos-2 scope mixture}: mean task MASE 1.058165, or
\good{1.03\% lower} than univariate Chronos-2. It is only 0.08\% below native
multivariate Chronos-2, so the practical difference between those two means is
small. Per-variate and task selection improve on univariate by 0.83\% and
0.49\%, respectively. The standalone retrieved-covariate candidates are all
worse than univariate, and increasing $K$ from 1 to 20 adds substantial
recorded inference cost without an aggregate accuracy gain.

Chronos-Bolt univariate has mean task MASE 1.167905, 9.23\% above univariate
Chronos-2. The direct-horizon mixture is not usable as an aggregate method: one
SG\_Carpark task reaches MASE 313,637 with Chronos-2 and 382,465 with Bolt,
dominating both 90-task means. The remaining findings are not driven by this
method.

\section{Question and protocol}

For an observed target history $x$ and horizon-$H$ future $y$, a frozen
backbone maps its supplied input representation to a median point forecast
$\widehat y$. Selectime asks whether a held-out validation period can choose or
mix useful inputs without training model parameters and without observing the
official test future.

Chronos-2 compares univariate, native multivariate, past-only
$\sqrt{|x|}$/sign augmentation, and retrieved covariates for
$K\in\{1,5,10,15,20\}$. It also reports task and per-variate candidate
selection, binary scope selection, a frozen univariate/multivariate mixture, a
frozen univariate/top-5 mixture, and a direct-neighbor-horizon mixture.
Chronos-Bolt reports univariate forecasts and the direct-horizon mixture.

Validation choices use mean squared scaled error and are frozen before test.
Validation rows with no finite available target history are excluded before
backbone inference; if no usable rows remain, selection keeps univariate input.
Soft mixtures use a Beta$(1,1)$ smoothed validation win frequency with half
ties and exclude fallback rows. Retrieval uses instance-normalized lookback
distance, aligned historical windows, and lookback-only rescaling. Candidate
fallbacks use the canonical univariate forecast. The comparison uses seed 0
once. Each method has 67,502 finite MASE cells on the same Seasonal-defined
grid; the reported mean gives equal weight to each of 90 task means.

\section{What validation selected}

The task selector keeps univariate input on 39 of 90 tasks, chooses
multivariate on 31, self-augmentation on nine, and retrieval on eleven. Its
retrieval choices include four $K=20$ tasks. The 3,882 per-variate decisions
use univariate input 51.4\% of the time and multivariate input 22.3\%; all
retrieval choices together account for 14.9\%.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../outputs/analysis/expanded_results_20260919/selection_frequencies.pdf}
\caption{Frozen validation choices for the original task and per-variate
selectors. These frequencies describe selected representations, not causal
contributions to accuracy.}
\end{figure}

Every standalone retrieved-$K$ method uses univariate fallback on 10,293 cells
(15.25\%). The task and per-variate selectors fall back on 6 and 440 cells;
the top-5 mixture falls back on 3,032 cells. Complete metric coverage therefore
does not imply successful retrieval everywhere.

\section{Accuracy and recorded cost}

The summed Chronos-2 candidate query-loop times are 235 seconds for
univariate, 247 for multivariate, 736 for self-augmentation, and 433, 1,406,
2,710, 3,950 and 5,279 seconds for $K=1,5,10,15,20$. These are not end-to-end
times: retrieval and datastore preprocessing are recorded separately, and
selected methods reuse candidate artifacts rather than having an independently
measured latency.

\section{Direct-horizon instability}

The direct-horizon method rescales a retrieved observed continuation using
neighbor and query lookback standard deviations, then mixes it with the
univariate forecast. Its Chronos-2/Bolt aggregate MASE is 3,485.98/4,250.80.
Excluding only SG\_Carpark/15T/short reduces those means to 1.1471/1.2139,
showing that one extreme task dominates, although even the reduced means do
not beat the corresponding vanilla forecasts.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../outputs/analysis/expanded_results_20260919/direct_horizon_instability.pdf}
\caption{Sorted direct-horizon-mixture task MASE. The log scale exposes the
single six-order-of-magnitude failure while retaining the other tasks.}
\end{figure}

The compact synchronized evidence proves the extreme finite errors and the
unbounded rescaling rule, but it does not include raw neighbor or prediction
arrays. The exact offending neighbor/channel cannot therefore be reconstructed
locally. The supported conclusion is that this direct-horizon formulation is
numerically unsafe as evaluated; attributing the outlier to a specific
near-constant lookback remains a plausible mechanism, not an observed fact.

\section{Limits}

The report manifests and compact task summaries agree with the expanded
scientific contract. Raw prediction, retrieval and metric arrays were not
independently inspected. One seed provides no repeat robustness or significance
assessment. Population standard deviations describe within-task metric cells,
not uncertainty across runs. Selected-method latency remains undefined. These
limits do not change the observed ordering, but they constrain claims about
generalization, uncertainty and execution cost.
